1. Scope and responsibility

This Privacy Policy describes the personal information processed through Gezopia Keyboard (“Application”), the purposes of that processing, the recipients of information and the choices and rights available to you. Wubliker Asrat (“Developer”, “we”, “us”) is responsible for the Application and for information received directly through support communications.

The Application distinguishes local keyboard operations from optional online services. It does not require a Gezopia user account. Ordinary keyboard input is processed on your device, not uploaded to a Developer-operated typing service. Optional AI, translation, image and voice operations may transmit content to an external provider. We do not operate a relay for those provider requests.

Other applications receive content you enter or share with them. Their processing, and processing independently performed by Android, backup services, email services and selected online providers, is governed by their respective policies. This Policy does not represent that the Developer controls those independent parties.

This Policy provides information; it does not itself obtain consent. Acceptance of Terms, keyboard activation and Android permissions do not substitute for any separate consent required for a particular processing activity.

2. Categories of information and purposes

2.1 Keyboard input and personalization. The Application processes characters and surrounding input context made available by the receiving application to enter text, provide suggestions and perform supported editing functions. It may retain learned words, dictionary information, preferences and typing statistics locally to personalize keyboard behavior. Ordinary typing and the local learned dictionary are not sent to us for advertising, profiling or model training.

Learning depends on your settings, incognito controls and the field information supplied by the receiving application. Learning is restricted for fields identified as sensitive or private. This protection depends in part on the receiving application correctly identifying those fields. Incognito controls for keyboard learning do not make an explicitly requested external AI or voice operation anonymous.

2.2 Clipboard information. Where Android permits clipboard access, the Application can read clipboard changes and maintain local text history for clipboard features. This version ignores image clipboard items and does not import, retain, preview or paste clipboard images; MIME information may be inspected to skip them. Access is not limited to tapping Paste. Clipboard content may contain personal information copied from other applications. It is used to display and manage history and carry out requested insertion or sharing; clipboard history is not automatically uploaded to the Developer. Content deliberately submitted to an online tool or shared with another application is subject to that separate operation. Clipboard history ingestion pauses while the keyboard’s incognito mode is enabled; existing saved history remains until removed. Sensitivity protection also depends on whether the copying app supplies Android’s sensitive-clip flag.

2.3 Online requests and generated results. Optional tools process prompts, relevant conversation history, translations, generated output and selected attachments to perform the operation you request. Attachments can include images, documents and text containing information about you or other persons. Saved conversations and retained attachments can remain in private app storage to support history and later access.

2.4 Credentials and configuration. Provider names, endpoint addresses, model choices and API credentials are stored to configure and authenticate requested services. Credential checks and model-list retrieval send requests to the configured provider even before a conversation is submitted. A provider may associate these requests with your external account.

2.5 Audio and transcripts. Voice features process microphone audio for transcription and process recognized text for supported text translation or refinement. Audio may contain other persons’ speech or sensitive information. The Application does not offer a floating or cross-app voice session and has no capability to display content over other apps; voice capture runs only inside the keyboard or the Application’s own screens. Audio capture and transcript retention are separate activities.

2.6 Imported files. Themes, fonts, sounds and other selected materials are read and, where needed, retained locally to apply your customization or carry out import, export or sharing. System pickers provide access to selected files; the Application does not require general access to your entire photo or file library for these imports. App-owned temporary sharing copies and export mirrors may be cleaned up when they are older than 24 hours during later exports. This is bounded, best-effort cleanup, not a timer: copies can remain longer when cleanup is unavailable. Cleanup does not traverse the folder you choose for your saved exports or delete source themes.

2.7 Diagnostics and support. The Application saves a limited number of fatal error reports locally for troubleshooting. New reports use the same category-only exception sanitization as our Java/Kotlin reporting boundary, removing original exception messages and using a fixed thread label. They can contain application/version information and filtered stack frames. Older locally retained reports may still contain unsanitized exception messages. The Application does not automatically send these local reports to a Developer-operated crash service. If you email us, we receive your email address, message, attachments and associated information supplied by your email service, to respond, investigate the issue and handle your request.

3. Optional providers and network recipients

3.1 Selected AI and speech providers. Requests are sent directly from your device to the configured service, such as Google Gemini, OpenAI, Addis AI or a custom compatible endpoint. Depending on the operation, that service receives prompts, relevant conversation context, attachments, audio or transcript text, together with authentication and connection information. A provider can receive your IP address, request time, model selection and other information necessary to deliver its service.

A text-translation operation may transmit recognized text to a text model. A continuous live-translation operation is not offered by the Application. Do not assume all translation is text-only or that all microphone use is local. An OpenAI-compatible service is not necessarily operated by OpenAI.

Providers determine their own retention, human-review, improvement/training, account, billing and deletion practices under their applicable policies and your arrangement with them. These practices can differ by provider, endpoint, subscription, region and account settings. No universal promise that providers never store or train on submitted content is made by this Policy. Supplying your own key does not make an online request local.

3.2 Google handwriting services. Handwriting recognition uses Google ML Kit Digital Ink. Recognition runs on the device using downloaded models. Model delivery requires communication with Google. Google's ML Kit disclosure describes SDK technical-data processing for diagnostics and usage analytics, including device and application information, installation-related identifiers, performance metrics, API configuration, feature events and errors, and configured languages for Digital Ink. On-device recognition does not mean that the SDK makes no technical-data requests.

3.3 Capability information. Relevant translation features can periodically retrieve provider-capability information hosted on GitHub. These refresh requests can occur when a feature initializes or its cached information becomes stale, without a conversation Send action. The refresh does not include your prompt or API key; the hosting service receives connection information such as your IP address.

3.4 Other recipients. Information you insert, paste, export or share is provided to the receiving app or recipient. Files obtained through a system picker, external camera operation or document provider may also be handled by that service. Support email is handled by the email services involved. App distribution and Android backup services process information under their own policies.

3.5 Dashboard announcements. When you open the dashboard, Firebase Remote Config checks for developer announcements such as app updates or new themes. The app caches the configuration and normally checks for changes at most once an hour. Google receives installation-related identifiers, app/device and connection information to provide this service. We do not include Firebase Analytics or send typing, clipboard contents, AI prompts or audio as configuration parameters. Announcements appear inside the dashboard only, not in the Android status bar or while typing. Closing a message saves its identifier locally to avoid repeatedly displaying it. No reply, account or notification permission is required. Dashboard messages are independent of the optional status-bar announcements below.

3.6 Firebase stability reports. The Application automatically uses Google Firebase Crashlytics for Java/Kotlin stability reports, separate from the local log files in section 2.7. Reports can include app/release, device/OS and session information, SDK-generated installation identifiers, stack traces and SDK-supported diagnostic information such as Android application-not-responding information. These identifiers are not anonymous. We do not set a custom user ID, add typing/clipboard contents, prompts or audio, or enable screenshots, session replay, Analytics breadcrumbs or native crash reporting.

For Java/Kotlin fatal errors handled by our application boundary, exception messages and suppressed exceptions are removed, cause depth and frame counts are bounded, and static failure categories plus stack frames are passed to Crashlytics instead of the original throwable. This restriction does not claim that every SDK/OS-generated diagnostic field can be filtered by that boundary. Raw local crash-log files are not uploaded by this integration. Google processes diagnostic information under its Firebase policies, including https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy, with retention and deletion limitations described there. Automatic stability reporting is separate from Android notification permission and advertising consent. Please contact us using the privacy contact below regarding this processing.

3.7 Optional status-bar announcements. Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) can deliver occasional developer update notifications separately from dashboard messages. Android notification permission, application notification settings and notification channels control their display. On Android 13 and newer, the app asks for notification permission once at the first eligible dashboard opening after setup, before displaying dashboard news. Declining does not block dashboard messages or typing, and ordinary later openings do not repeat the request. On Android versions where notifications are allowed by default, delivery registration can start without an additional permission dialog; on Android 13 and newer it starts after notification permission is granted, including a permission previously granted for voice-session controls. Settings includes a Notifications switch for developer update alerts; turning it off does not stop voice-session recording controls. Both this preference and Android permission must allow update notifications. FCM processes installation/registration identifiers, app/device and connection information for message delivery. The app does not supply typing, clipboard contents, AI prompts or audio as messaging data. Turning the in-app switch off gates announcement display and requests unsubscribe/token deletion immediately; blocking notifications through Android blocks display there, and the next app launch/resume disables messaging automatic initialization and requests the same cleanup. Cleanup needs connectivity and already-started SDK operations may complete later. Google processing and retention are subject to its Firebase policies. No Firebase Analytics or BigQuery message-delivery export is enabled.

3.8 Closed-test access checks. Only specially enabled closed-test builds use Firebase Remote Config to retrieve one shared test-access calendar window, and contact https://www.google.com/generate_204 over HTTPS to check its response date. These checks can occur at startup and when the dashboard or keyboard opens, without a Send action. Google receives ordinary connection information, including your IP address and request time; Remote Config also processes the installation-related information described above. We do not send typing, clipboard contents, prompts, audio or a custom tester identifier with the time request. Verified time is advanced using the device's elapsed-time clock, not its calendar setting. A reference to that verified time and the shared window is encrypted and authenticated using an Android Keystore key in app-private, backup-excluded storage. It can restore temporary access after an app restart only within the same phone boot and only until the fixed shared cutoff. A phone reboot, reinstall, cleared app data, or an unreadable reference requires fresh internet verification; ordinary typing and free or authentically earned theme access remain available without that verification. Clearing the app cache does not clear this reference. The reference is separate from earned points and theme ownership. No separate tester account, activation server or billing service is used. Normal public builds do not perform these test-access checks.

4. Advertising, sale and developer analytics

The Application integrates Google AdMob (Google Mobile Ads SDK) to offer optional rewarded ads in dashboard theme-unlock features. Ads are not displayed in the keyboard while you type. Completing an eligible rewarded presentation adds progress toward the selected theme; it does not create a transferable cash balance. Local theme progress and unlock records are stored on your device.

Google's advertising SDK can collect and share your IP address (which may indicate approximate location), device and advertising identifiers, app/ad interactions such as taps and video views, and diagnostic information for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. Processing depends on SDK behavior, device settings, region and applicable consent choices. Ordinary typing, learned words, clipboard history, AI prompts and microphone content are not intentionally supplied by the Application to AdMob as ad-request content.

The Application uses Google's User Messaging Platform to request applicable privacy choices and determine ad-request eligibility. Where required and configured, a Google privacy message provides choices about processing for advertising. Available advertising privacy choices can be revisited through the Application's Settings privacy-options control. Android advertising settings let you reset or delete your advertising ID. These controls do not guarantee that all technical processing stops or that no ads will be shown. You may decline to watch optional rewarded ads and continue ordinary keyboard use; themes requiring an unearned reward remain locked.

Google processes advertising information under its policies and applicable arrangements, including its own retention and international-processing practices. We do not sell ordinary typing or clipboard history to data brokers or operate a Developer-hosted keystroke analytics service. Firebase stability reporting is described separately in section 3.6. Advertising SDK analytics and fraud-prevention processing are separate from the local diagnostic reports described above.

5. Permissions and access

The Application uses the following Android capabilities for the purposes indicated:

  • Input-method activation: to act as your selected keyboard and enter text into the receiving application. Activation is not permission to transmit all typed content to online providers.
  • Internet and network-state access: for provider requests, model delivery, capability refreshes, advertising and consent services.
  • Advertising ID: for Google advertising SDK processing, subject to Android settings and applicable privacy choices.
  • Microphone: for explicit in-app or keyboard voice transcription you initiate.
  • Notifications: for optional status-bar developer update alerts, subject to Android settings. Dashboard popups do not use this permission.
  • Vibration: for optional keyboard haptics.
  • System file and photo pickers: for selected imports and attachments. Supported camera capture is delegated to an external camera application.

The Application does not request location or contacts permissions for keyboard operation, nor broad photo/storage access. Content you choose to submit can nevertheless contain location, contact or other sensitive information.

Microphone access requires permission and an explicit start action. The Application starts voice capture only through the in-app or keyboard voice controls you use; it does not offer a floating session that continues while you use another app. Use the relevant Stop, Finish or leave-feature controls, and stop other active recordings if necessary. Revoking access can disable the corresponding feature.

6. Storage and security

Local information is kept in application storage, but different categories have different protection. Learned-word databases use application-level database encryption. Provider credentials use encrypted preferences where available; if that store cannot be initialized, the Application can fall back to private, unencrypted preferences. Saved dashboard conversations and retained attachments are ordinary files in private app storage, not universally application-encrypted files.

The Application’s microphone capture implementation does not save raw microphone recordings as local audio files. Audio is buffered in memory for capture/transmission; this does not prevent an external provider from retaining information it receives.

Private app storage and device protections reduce unauthorized access but do not guarantee confidentiality on a compromised device, after sharing, or within an independent service. Use trusted endpoints and secure network connections. This Policy does not describe transport encryption as end-to-end encryption, nor guarantee that every custom endpoint has equivalent security. No storage or transmission method is entirely secure.

7. Android backup and device transfer

Depending on Android version, device settings and account configuration, eligible preferences and files may be backed up or transferred by the operating system. Eligible files include saved dashboard conversations and retained chat attachments. Imported customization files may also be eligible. Such information is not necessarily confined to the current physical device.

The Application’s backup rules exclude the specified provider credential stores and the clipboard preference store. Learned-word databases are excluded from the configured cloud-backup scope and device-transfer rules. Local crash files are excluded from cloud backup and device transfer; other files may transfer if not separately excluded. New text-clipboard history writes use a separate Android Keystore AES-GCM encrypted file in app-private, backup-excluded storage. On upgrade, the app copies existing text history to that encrypted store and verifies the saved copy before removing the old plaintext history preference. If encryption or verification fails, the legacy data is retained rather than reset; new changes are not written to a plaintext fallback. Failed legacy cleanup is retried after later verified reads or writes, so an older plaintext copy may remain until cleanup succeeds. An unreadable encrypted file is not replaced or merged with stale legacy history. When secure storage is unavailable, changes held in memory may not survive an app restart; the app shows a storage warning. Clearing a preference is logical deletion, not a guarantee of forensic erasure from flash storage. Clipboard image-history metadata retained from older versions is left untouched on the device, but this version no longer reads or uses that store. The configured backup and device-transfer rules now exclude that legacy metadata store; this does not remove copies that may already exist in older backups. Source images are not deleted.

System backup retention and deletion are controlled by the relevant platform and account settings. Deleting local data does not itself delete historical backups. Restoring eligible data can restore information previously saved by the Application.

8. Retention and deletion

Retention depends on the category and feature:

  • Preferences, learned information and customization remain until changed, removed through available controls or removed by clearing app data or uninstalling, subject to system backup and restore.
  • Clipboard history is retained according to its history and pinning controls until removed or replaced by those controls or app-data removal.
  • Keyboard Studio applies a seven-day inactivity retention policy when its conversation policy is evaluated. This is not a universal deletion timer for dashboard chats, attachments, backups or provider records.
  • Saved dashboard conversations and associated retained attachments support saved history. Deletion and attachment cleanup operate through the relevant feature and storage rules; the Policy does not promise instantaneous removal of every cached or unreferenced file.
  • The Application keeps up to five local fatal error reports, replacing older reports as new ones are saved.
  • Support correspondence is retained as needed to address the request, investigate related issues and satisfy applicable legal or dispute requirements. Retention depends on the correspondence and any obligations applying to it; deletion requests can be made to the privacy contact.

Android Settings → Apps → Gezopia Keyboard → Storage → Clear data, or uninstalling the Application, removes local application data subject to platform behavior. Clearing cache alone does not remove all saved information. Removing a provider key does not revoke that key at the provider.

Local deletion does not erase copies held by providers, email recipients, receiving applications or backups. Direct provider-held information requests to the relevant provider as well. We cannot remotely inspect or erase device-only information that has not been sent to us.
